Only a course's named reviewer publishes its revisions. A published revision never changes, and every completion stays bound to the revision it was made against.

## Publish or return a revision

Revisions waiting for you appear under **Waiting for my review** on **My learning**. Open one to read every lesson and the assessment as learners will see them.

To publish, choose what happens **When published**. **If you move open assignments** above the form shows, before you publish, who would move, which lessons each keeps or reopens, what happens to their attempts, and who has finished an earlier revision:

- **Keep current assignments on their revision**: people finish the revision they started.
- **Move open assignments to this revision, keeping unchanged progress**: people still working move to the new revision. Lessons whose title, text and sources did not change stay done; changed and new lessons open again. If the quiz and practical are unchanged, attempts carry over (a pass stays a pass, and a practical waiting for its assessor still counts); if they changed, earlier attempts no longer count and an unfinished practical or timed quiz is voided, kept in the learner's history.
- **Move open assignments and reassign everyone who completed an earlier revision (a material change)**: as above, and people already done get a new assignment due in the number of days you set. Their earlier completions and certificates never change.

Set the **Days to complete a reassignment**, add a note if you like, and press **Publish this revision**.

To send it back instead, write **What needs changing** and press **Return to author**. Earlier completions stay bound to the revision they were made against.

## Authors, reviewer handovers and self-review

A course's page has **Authors and reviewer**. The owner, or the learning administrator of the course's business unit, ticks **Course authors** to let someone write that one course and send it for review without the author role; they see no other course, no people administration and no learner evidence. **Replace the reviewer** hands the course to a new named reviewer with a reason; the handover is listed there and every earlier decision keeps the reviewer who made it.

Nobody decides their own revision unless the owner presses **Allow self-review** on that course. Then the person who submitted it may decide it (a business unit's learning administrator can be the named reviewer for this), and the revision is labelled **self-reviewed** in the course's history. **Turn self-review off** refuses it again.

## When a source changes

When an author uploads a new version of a source, every course whose published lessons cite the earlier version shows **Source changed** to its reviewer, naming the old and new version numbers. Read the change and record your decision:

- **No material change to the training**: the course stays as it is.
- **Training needs updating (the draft is being revised)**: the author updates the lessons and submits a new revision, which you publish with the reassignment you choose.

## Proposed changes

An author's agent can propose changes to a course draft, with the source passages it relies on and anything it could not find. They appear on the course page as **Proposed change**: **Apply to draft** or **Decline**. Applying changes only the draft; nothing is published until a reviewer publishes a revision.
