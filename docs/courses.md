A course in Beam is built from your own procedures. Every lesson cites the exact version of the documents it teaches, so when a document changes, Beam knows which courses to look at again.

## Add a source

A source is a procedure or other material your business owns or may use: a handbook, a checklist, a short video. Open **Sources** and use **Add a source**:

- **Title** and **File**.
- **License**: owned by the business, licensed to the business, or used with written permission, with the **license evidence**.
- **Where it came from**, so anyone can trace it.
- The **responsible reviewer**.

Every version is kept unchanged. When the document changes, open it and use **Upload a new version**. Courses that cite the earlier version are flagged for their reviewer (see [Review and publish](/docs/review#when-a-source-changes)).

## Create a course

Open **Courses** and use **New course**: give it a **Title**, an optional **Summary**, and the **Reviewer who publishes it**. You land on the course's draft.

### Lessons

Use **Add a lesson** with a **Lesson title** and **Lesson text**, and tick the source versions it **cites**. Lesson text is plain text with a little structure:

- A blank line starts a new paragraph.
- Lines starting with `- ` make a list, and lines starting with `1. `, `2. ` and so on make numbered steps.
- A line starting with `# ` is a heading.
- When a lesson opens with a one-line paragraph, Beam highlights it for the learner: put the line that matters first.

Open a lesson in the draft to edit it, **Move up**, or **Remove lesson**.

### Quiz

Use **Add a question**: write the **Question**, the **Choices, one per line**, and the **Correct choice numbers**, such as `2`, or `1,3` when several are correct. A question scores only when exactly the right choices are picked, so scoring is the same every time.

Under **Add a question** you can also pick **Fill in the blank** (write `___` where each blank goes, then one line of **Accepted answers** per blank with alternatives separated by `|`, and tick **Ignore upper and lower case** if you want; spaces at either end never count), **Ordering** (the items one per line in the right order) or **Matching** (one `left = right` pair per line). Learners fill each blank, choose an item for each position or a match for each item from labelled lists (on matching questions they can also drag each answer onto its item, or tap the answer and then the item), and a question scores only when every blank, position or pair is right.

Pick **Written answer** for a free-response question: learners type their answer and the course's **Named assessor** grades it. **Guidance for the assessor** is never shown to learners. Every question has **Points**, its weight in the score (1 unless you change it): a question earns all its points only when its whole answer is right, and a written answer earns what the assessor awards, from 0 to its points. Tick **Always ask this question** for questions every attempt must include.

### Assessment rules

Under **Assessment rules**, set the **Pass mark (%)** and the number of **Quiz attempts allowed**. The course's questions are its question bank: **Questions drawn per attempt** makes each attempt ask that many, always-asked questions first and the rest picked at random, and **Shuffle the order for each attempt** mixes their order. Each attempt keeps the questions it asked, and reopening an attempt never draws again. **Learners see which answers were right** sets when learners see which questions they got right: after each attempt, once they pass or have no attempts left, or never (they see only the score). For practical work, write the **Practical assessment instructions**. The **Named assessor** decides practical evidence and grades written answers. Leave the instructions empty for a course without a practical part.

### Satisfaction survey

Under **Satisfaction survey**, write one question per line. Start a line with `rating:` to ask for a rating from 1 to 5. Learners may answer once, after every lesson is done. Answers are kept without names, never count toward passing or completion, and appear on the revision page only once at least three learners have answered.

## Send for review

**Submit for review** freezes the draft as a new revision for the course's reviewer. You can keep editing the draft; the revision in review does not change. The reviewer publishes it or returns it with a note: see [Review and publish](/docs/review).

## Retire or copy a course

On a course's page, **Retire this course** with a reason when it should no longer be assigned, for example when a newer course replaces it. Nobody can assign it again, through Manage, a team, a role path or a renewal, until you press **Reinstate for new assignments**; people who already have it can still finish it, and its records, certificates and reports stay. **Copy into a new course** makes an unpublished course with the same lessons, quiz and reviewer, ready to edit and send for review.

## Organize the catalog

On a course's page, **Catalog details** sets its **Folder**, **Tags**, **Learning objectives**, **Language**, **Expected duration in minutes** and **Course owner**; tick **Offer this course as a template** to let authors **Start from a template** on **Courses**. On **Courses**, filter by **Folder** and **Tag**, use **Change tags on several courses** to add or remove tags in one go, and tick **Archived courses** to see courses you have archived. Only a retired course can be archived; it stays readable, and reinstating it brings it back.

## Working together on a draft

Several people can edit one course. If you save a lesson after someone else changed a different lesson, both changes are kept. If they changed the same lesson, your text is not lost: the course page shows **Conflicting edits** with both texts, and anyone editing the course chooses **Keep the current text**, uses yours, or saves a merged text.

Under each lesson and question, **Comment or request a change** starts a thread. Others reply, and **Resolve thread** closes it; a change request shows as requested until someone resolves it.

Learners can tell you about a lesson from the lesson page, as feedback or as something broken. Open reports appear under **Content feedback** on **Courses** for the course's owner (or its reviewer when it has no owner), its unit's learning administrator and the business owner, with the learner's name; other authors see them without the name. **Close** with a note on what was done; the learner sees it on the course.
