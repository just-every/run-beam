Everything a staff member needs is on **My learning**. Training that is part of their work is done in paid working time.

## My learning

- **Reminders**: training due soon or overdue, and messages a manager approved.
- **Assigned to me**: each course with its revision, lessons done and due date.
- **Completed**: every completion with its certificate.
- **Questions for you**: optional questions from a manager, when there are any.

Reviewers and assessors also see **Waiting for my review** and **Evidence and written answers to assess**.

## Lessons

Open a course and press **Start**, or **Resume** to carry on. Progress is saved as each lesson is marked done, so a person can start on a computer and finish on a phone. Each lesson links its **Source material**. **Mark done and continue** moves to the next lesson.

## Quiz and practical

When every lesson is done, **Take the quiz** opens. The pass mark, attempts left and each question's points are shown before starting. A question earns its points only when its whole answer is right. Written answers go to the named assessor, and the attempt is scored once they are graded. Depending on the course, an attempt may draw a different set of questions from the course's question bank, and which answers were right may show only once you pass or have used every attempt.

Some courses add an optional **Course survey**. Your answers are kept without your name and never affect passing or completion.

If the course has a practical part, **Submit evidence** describes what the person did, with an optional file, and sends it to the named assessor. The assessor accepts it or asks for resubmission with a note. Nobody can accept their own evidence.

## Practice

Some courses offer **Practice**: short scenarios staff can try as often as they like. Practice never counts toward completion and nobody scores it.

## Certificates

Every completion issues a numbered certificate from your business, naming the course revision. Use the browser's print command to save a copy. A certificate records completion of your own training; it is not an accreditation or a statement of competence.

## The course catalog

Open **Catalog** to find courses offered to you. Search, or narrow by subject, skill, role, language and how long a course takes. **Enroll** starts an open course straight away; a course that needs approval has **Request enrollment**, and your manager approves or declines it. Work someone assigned you is required; courses you chose are optional, due in 90 days, and **Withdraw** leaves one while keeping your progress on record. Favorites and saved searches are yours alone.

Whoever manages a course's catalog details sets how it is offered under **Catalog enrollment** on the course page: only by assignment, open to anyone it is offered to, or on approval, for everyone or chosen teams.

## Sessions

Some courses meet in a classroom or online. Open **Sessions** to see the sessions of courses assigned to you and **Choose this session**; if the course comes after another one on your path, finish that one first. You hold one session per course: **Switch to this session** moves you, and **Leave session** gives up your place. Your agenda lists each meeting at the session's own local time, where it is, who leads it and what to bring, and tells you when a session is moved, changed or cancelled. **Add to calendar (.ics)** downloads the session for your calendar program; after a change, download it again and your calendar updates the same events. Beam does not email invitations yet.

When a session is full, **Join the waitlist**: Sessions shows your place in line. When a seat opens, the session either gives it to the next person straight away or offers it for a set time; an offer shows on My learning and Sessions with **Accept the seat** and **Decline**, and passes to the next person if it is not accepted in time. A session may close registration at a set date and time. Your session history lists each step.

The owner and learning administrators schedule sessions on the same page: a classroom location or a join link, the instructor, materials, and one meeting or a daily or weekly series at the same local time, which stays at that time across daylight-saving changes. Under **Seats and waitlist** they set the number of seats, the registration deadline, whether a freed seat is offered or given, how long an offer lasts and whether the waitlist goes by joining order or by whose assignment is due soonest; **Roster history** lists every registration, waitlist and offer step.

A course can require attending a session: its author sets the share of the session's time under **Assessment rules**, and the reviewer publishes it with the revision. Once the session begins, its instructor (or whoever scheduled it) opens **Roster and attendance** and records each person as attended, partly attended (with the minutes) or absent, with a decision, a note and an optional evidence file. Mistakes are fixed with **Amend** and a reason; the timeline keeps every record. The course completes when its lessons, any assessment and the attendance are all done.

## Telling the course team

At the end of a lesson, **Tell us about this lesson** sends feedback, or reports something broken, to the people who look after the course. Only your words are sent, never your quiz answers or scores. The course page lists what you sent and, once it is closed, what was done.
