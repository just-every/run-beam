# Beam product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Beam is a complete SMB employee LMS that turns
company procedures, licensed compliance libraries and existing course packages
into maintained role-based onboarding, practice, compliance and renewal programs.
It is the product a US employer can switch to from TalentLMS, Trainual, Docebo
or iSpring Learn, with the collaborative, AI and mobile learning buyers seek
from 360Learning, Sana (Workday) and Arist.

This contract describes the product being built towards. The final Status
table separates implemented behavior from incomplete and missing work; a gap
does not narrow the destination. Each clause includes an observable acceptance
journey through screens, forms, JSON APIs, downloads, commands or authenticated
provider callbacks. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same journeys and statuses. Paid
or network services may be exercised with their real sandboxes, with test fakes
only at that boundary; no application path simulates delivery, AI or learning.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Employee training software / learning management system (LMS) |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | TalentLMS, Trainual, Docebo, iSpring Learn, 360Learning, Sana (Workday), Arist |
| First buyer | An operations or people lead at a 20–200 employee US employer with repeat onboarding, multi-state compliance learning and changing procedures; employer and provider costs are in USD |

**The whole job.** Maintain the workforce learning directory, author and review
courses, play the employer’s existing packages, enroll the right people from
HR, deliver learning on web and native mobile (including offline), assess
understanding and observed practical work, run live/blended programs, deliver
follow-up and automatic renewals, and retain/export reliable evidence. Named
connections below are obligations, even where provider app approval, contracts,
content licensing or native app distribution require engineering work.

**Products keep their separation.** Beam owns employee learning and its optional
customer/partner learning portals. [Slate](https://github.com/just-every/run-slate)
owns the company knowledge library; Beam connects to source procedures and
learning citations rather than replacing it. [Willow](https://github.com/just-every/run-willow)
and connected HRIS/payroll products own worker records, leave and employment
decisions. Beam consumes authorized fields and writes learning outcomes only:
revision-bound completions go back to the HRIS training record (CONNECT-017).
It does not absorb unowned category gaps or turn into a course-selling platform.

**Evidence and authority.** Progress, completion and competence are separate.
Native/package completion records the exact assigned course and assessment or
package revision with its method and evidence. Imported history is labeled and
does not silently satisfy current learning. Practice, attendance, message
delivery and signatures do not become assessment passes. Published versions
and old completions remain immutable. Reviewers retain publication/materiality
and assessors retain practical decisions; every handler, API, file and mobile
door enforces the current role and data scope. Agents prepare within explicit
grants, never approve their own work or administer people. Uploaded documents,
packages and operational text are untrusted data, not model instructions.

## Published clauses

The original BEAM-01 through BEAM-20 promises remain intact as the implemented
native learning foundation. Expanded target clauses add obligations rather
than claiming these narrower proofs already cover the full target.

### Published native learning guarantees

- **BEAM-01 — Configure the employer and staff access.** One business with a name and IANA time zone, configured by the owner before anything else. Adult staff are invited by the owner through ordinary emailed sign-in, hold author, reviewer, learner, manager and assessor roles, and belong to teams. Removing access ends every session; restoring it needs a fresh sign-in. Roles are enforced on every action and read. **Acceptance evidence:** Through setup, invitation and people forms, name the employer and zone, invite each role, remove access during an active session, restore it and require a fresh sign-in; try the same actions as a learner.

- **BEAM-02 — Keep owned source versions safely.** Owned source documents and media are uploaded with license basis and evidence, provenance and a responsible reviewer. Bytes are checksummed, verified after storage and on every read; versions are immutable. Learners open only material cited by training assigned to them. Uploaded content is inert: never rendered as HTML or script. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload a licensed procedure and replacement version through Sources, compare stored/downloaded digests, refuse unlicensed material, and try to open an unassigned source as a learner; uploaded script stays inert.

- **BEAM-03 — Publish reviewed immutable course revisions.** Courses have one editable draft with version-checked saves (a stale save is refused, never merged). Submitting freezes an immutable revision; only the course's named reviewer (or the human owner) publishes or returns it. Publication uses the private human session and publication form; JSON publication is refused regardless of cookie or body encoding. Publishing supersedes the previous revision; later draft edits never change a published revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Edit and submit a course through Courses, refuse a stale save and an unrelated reviewer, publish by the named reviewer’s human form, refuse JSON publication and confirm later draft edits leave the published revision unchanged.

- **BEAM-04 — Build versioned role paths.** Role paths list a team's required courses with due days, prerequisites and renewal intervals. Saving a path creates a new path revision; assignments keep the revision they came from. A learner cannot start a course until its prerequisite is completed. Assigning a path twice creates no duplicates. **Acceptance evidence:** Save and revise a role path through Paths, assign it twice, and attempt the next course before and after its prerequisite; inspect the original assignment’s retained path revision.

- **BEAM-05 — Assign individuals and teams.** Assignments go to individuals and teams with a due date, time and explicit IANA time zone, and bind the course's published revision. One open assignment per learner and course. Invalid dates and zones are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Assign a published course through Manage to one person and a team, repeat it, and try invalid calendar dates and zones; inspect one open assignment per person/course with its precise revision and due instant.

- **BEAM-06 — Resume native learning across devices.** Lesson progress is stored on the server; a learner resumes at the next unread lesson from any device or session, at desktop and phone widths. **Acceptance evidence:** Read one lesson through My learning, sign in in another browser/device session and resume at the next unread lesson at desktop and phone widths.

- **BEAM-07 — Score quizzes and control retries.** Deterministic quizzes: a question scores only when exactly the correct choices are selected; the pass mark is a percentage. Every attempt keeps its responses, score, scoring rule and retry decision. When attempts run out a manager may grant one more, with a reason. **Acceptance evidence:** Take correct, partly correct and failing quiz attempts through the learner page, exhaust retries, and have a manager grant a reasoned extra attempt; inspect stored responses and scoring rules.

- **BEAM-08 — Decide practical evidence through the named assessor.** Practical evidence (text and an optional file) goes to the assessment's named assessor; only that person with the current assessor role can read and accept or reject it (the learner and managers retain read access), and nothing completes before the decision. Removing the role immediately removes decision, queue and evidence access from existing sessions. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit text and a file through Practical evidence, try an unrelated assessor, revoke the named assessor’s role in an existing session, then restore and decide; completion waits for the authorized decision.

- **BEAM-09 — Keep exact revision completion evidence.** Native learning completion is recorded only when every lesson of the assigned revision is done and each required assessment is passed or accepted, and binds the exact course and assessment revision with its evidence. Offline completion uses the separate instructor attestation in BEAM-10. Every completion linked to an assignment must match its learner, course and exact course revision. No door turns partial progress into a completion. Publishing a new revision, with or without reassignment, leaves earlier completions bound to their revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Attempt premature completion, finish the required lessons and assessments through learner doors, publish a newer course revision and compare the old completion’s exact course/assessment revision and evidence.

- **BEAM-10 — Record instructor-attested offline training.** Managers and assessors record instructor-attested offline training (date, instructor, location, attestation, optional evidence file) against an explicitly selected current or historical published revision; it closes only assignments for the same learner, course and exact revision and records its provenance. Another revision's open assignment stays open; that attendance has its own completion and certificate. Future dates are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Record attendance through Manage for current and historical published revisions, reject future dates and verify only a matching revision’s assignment closes while the other remains open.

- **BEAM-11 — Issue numbered completion certificates.** Every completion issues a numbered certificate naming the issuer (the business), learner, course revision, date and optional expiry, stating that it is not an accreditation or a statement of competence. Learners see their own; managers see all. **Acceptance evidence:** Complete a course, open its certificate as learner and manager, and verify its number, employer issuer, exact revision, date, expiry and non-accreditation statement.

- **BEAM-12 — Work reminders and decision queues.** In-app reminders for assignments due within three days or overdue; manager queues for overdue work, exhausted quiz attempts and renewals due within 30 days (assignable in one action); reviewer queues for revisions and source-change notices; assessor queues for practical evidence. Repeated renewals retain the completed assignment's interval, path revision and prerequisite even after the path changes; each certificate keeps that expiry policy. One latest completion per learner/course is considered, including when offline dates tie, and no renewal duplicates an open assignment. **Acceptance evidence:** Open learner reminders and each role’s queue with due, overdue, retry, source-change, practical and renewal work; repeat a renewal after a path change and verify no duplicate or lost original renewal policy.

- **BEAM-13 — Protect each learner’s private records.** A learner cannot read another person's assignments, attempts, evidence files, records or certificates (answered as not found). Signed-out requests get nothing private. Cross-origin posts are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Request a second learner’s assignments, attempts, files, records and certificates through pages, downloads and JSON, then sign out and send a cross-origin write; each is refused without disclosure.

- **BEAM-14 — Maintain procedures with scoped preparation.** Procedure maintenance without a model account: a new source version flags every course whose published lessons cite an earlier version for its reviewer, who records whether the change is material. The owner's agent uses a preparation-only credential delegated from a live ordinary human session of an author, reviewer or manager: current access and roles, session revocation and expiry apply on every request, and it cannot publish, decide, administer or use human forms. A proposed draft (from a person, or the owner's own agent through the API) records the draft version and source versions it was prepared from, the source passages it relies on (each quoting one of those inputs) and the information it found missing; the reviewer sees both. It applies only to that unchanged draft and never to a published revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Change a source, inspect the reviewer’s affected-course notice, submit a passage-cited preparation proposal, reject an outdated draft proposal, revoke its delegated session and refuse agent publication or decision.

- **BEAM-15 — Move TalentLMS historical records in safely.** TalentLMS move-in kit (records first): course list CSV and course report workbooks (users overview and timeline). The preview writes nothing and reports matched and unresolved people, duplicates, unreadable rows, unmapped courses and gaps (certificate files, course content). Apply is bound to the reviewed fingerprint through Base's D1 import kit with a durable journal and retained originals. Imported completions stay labeled TalentLMS history and complete nothing current. Re-importing the same export adds nothing. Undo removes untouched imported rows and keeps rows a manager reviewed after import. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview a TalentLMS course-list CSV and users-overview/timeline workbooks in Import, verify no writes, resolve gaps, apply the fingerprint, repeat without duplicates and undo while preserving subsequently reviewed rows.

- **BEAM-16 — Export and restore the installation.** `rer export` writes every Beam table and stored file to an owner-only archive without sessions or credentials; import restores it into an empty fresh clone, refusing corrupt or non-empty targets; a second export is identical; records, files and certificates read back after a new sign-in. Export refuses to write into `public/`, and a build refuses to ship an archive copied there. **Acceptance evidence:** Use rer export, restore into an empty fresh installation, sign in anew and read records/files/certificates; reject corruption, a populated target and an archive placed under public assets; compare the next export.

- **BEAM-17 — Read and export the training report.** A manager reads a training report of every assignment and every completion recorded without one (offline attendance): learner, course and exact revision, status (open, overdue, completed, expiring within 30 days), due time in its zone, lessons done, quiz attempts and result, practical decision and assessor, completion, certificate and expiry. Course, team and status filters narrow it; the CSV download carries the same rows and keeps spreadsheet formulas inert. Learners are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Filter Reports by course, team and state, compare rows to the CSV download including offline completions and formula-like fields, and refuse report access to learners.

- **BEAM-18 — Approve prepared assignments and reminders.** The owner's agent prepares targeted assignment plans and reminder drafts for a manager's approval. Every proposal records the agent, the person whose credential it used, its inputs (source versions, course or path revisions, assignments, question plans), reasons, exclusions with reasons and its exact payload with that payload's SHA-256. Nothing reaches staff or assignments before a human approves; the agent cannot decide, people without the kind's authority cannot decide, and an approval names the payload it reviewed. Approval records who, when, the note and the result. An assignment plan binds the course revision it names and is refused, staying undecided, once a newer revision is published; approved reminders appear in-app to the learner of a still-open assignment only. **Acceptance evidence:** Issue a real preparation credential, propose assignments/reminders through the API, inspect reasons/exclusions and payload digest in Agent work, reject unauthorized or stale approvals and approve exact work as a manager.

- **BEAM-19 — Ask voluntary anonymous diagnostic questions.** Diagnose before assigning: the owner's agent prepares a question plan (purpose, questions, participants, closing date) and a manager approves it before anyone is asked. Answering is optional and once only; an invitee may decline. Answers are stored without the person or time; managers and their agent see participation counts, and the answers only after the plan closes with at least three answers, in no particular order. A process-change recommendation drawn from it is recorded when the manager approves it; Beam never turns answers into ratings. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve a question plan through Agent work, have invited staff answer once or decline, close it below and above the anonymity minimum, then inspect nameless answers and a separately approved process recommendation.

- **BEAM-20 — Practice reviewed branching scenarios.** The owner's agent prepares a text practice scenario for a published revision: situations, choices, feedback and a rubric, citing only sources the revision's lessons cite; a scenario with a dangling or unreachable step, or no ending, is refused. Only the course's named reviewer (or the owner) deploys it. A learner assigned that revision practices it from the course page, sees feedback after each choice and can start again; practice is never scored, never creates attempts, completions or certificates, and nobody else can open their runs. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare a source-cited scenario through the API, reject invalid graphs, deploy it as the named reviewer, practice/restart as the assigned learner and verify no quiz attempt, completion, certificate or other person’s run is exposed.

## Target clauses

### People, access and the learner catalog

- **ORG-001 — Maintain the employee learning directory.** People records carry a stable external employee id, preferred name, work email (optional: a person without one is identified by employee id and signs in under ORG-011), mobile number, preferred language, employment state, manager, role, department, location, hire date and custom fields. Email changes and rehires preserve the person’s prior learning without conflating two employees. Who reads and edits (ct, 19 Oct): the owner reads and edits every field for everyone; a unit’s learning administrator (ORG-002) does so for people in their units’ teams; a manager (ORG-004) reads, for people in their own teams, the employee id, name, preferred name, work email, role, department, location, employment state, manager and preferred language, plus a custom field only when the owner has switched on its “managers may read” (off by default), and edits nothing; the person reads their own whole record and edits nothing (preferred name and language edits wait for LEARN-017); a custom role (ORG-010) keeps the People list’s name, email, roles and teams; everyone else sees names only; agent credentials read no directory fields. The audit (OPS-001) records which fields changed, the employee id and work-email and employment moves, never a mobile number or a custom field’s value. A person’s sign-in account is linked to their record by account id, not by email. Changing the work email of someone who has signed in changes the directory email at once; their sign-in email changes only when they change it themselves after verifying the new address, and their page shows the sign-in email while the two differ. For someone whose invitation is still unused, the invitation goes to the new address and the old one no longer reaches them. A roster is a CSV file imported through People with an employee id and a name on every row, matched by employee id: a row whose email belongs to someone without an employee id gives them that id; a new id adds a learner, sent no invitation; a blank cell keeps the stored value (only an edit clears one); dates are YYYY-MM-DD. A leaver (employment state “terminated”) whose row is active again is rehired as the same person with their learning kept; access returns only when the owner restores it. A row whose employee id and email belong to two different people, or whose email belongs to someone with another employee id, changes nothing and waits under Roster conflicts until the owner or the unit’s learning administrator applies it (to the id’s holder, or as a new person, in both cases without the email) or dismisses it, each audited; an edit that gives a person an employee id someone else holds is refused. A custom role (ORG-010) may be limited to directory locations; column mapping, previews and bulk invitations are ORG-006’s. **Acceptance evidence:** Import and edit a US roster through People, including a person with an employee id and mobile number but no email, change an email, rehire an employee and introduce a conflicting external id; show retained history and a conflict requiring resolution.

- **ORG-002 — Delegate administration with explicit rights.** The owner controls installation, identity, providers, billing and administrator grants. A learning administrator manages the catalog, enrollment and reports within assigned business units, without gaining owner/provider authority; grants, expiry and revocation apply in every page, API and download. Within their units a learning administrator also manages team membership: they invite new people as learners into their units' teams and set those teams' members from people already in their units' teams. They never change a team outside their units, give a role other than learner, or draw in someone from outside their units; a person they remove from every team of their units returns to the owner, and an invitation they sent stops admitting once their grant ends. The access window of an enrollment a learning administrator created is theirs to set and extend (AUTO-009); course and path windows and other organization-wide access policy stay with the owner, and managers extend windows for people in their teams. The owner names team managers (ORG-004). Beam has no billing today, so the billing part of this clause holds vacuously; a future billing capability inherits it and stays the owner's. **Acceptance evidence:** Grant a learning administrator one unit, manage its courses and learners, then request another unit and provider credentials through HTML and JSON; deny both and revoke the active grant. As that administrator, invite a learner into the unit's team, set its members, set and extend the window of their own enrollment, and be refused another unit's team, another role, an outside person, naming a manager and another person's enrollment window.

- **ORG-003 — Scope authors and reviewers to their content.** Authors draft and request review only for authorized content; named reviewers publish or return it and choose retraining. Neither role administers people or sees unrelated learner evidence, and delegation/replacement of a reviewer is recorded without rewriting publication history. A one-person learning team works: when the owner allows self-review for a course, its learning administrator may be both author and named reviewer, and each such publication is recorded and reported as self-reviewed. **Acceptance evidence:** Give an expert one course, coauthor and review it, then try an unrelated course, people administration and evidence download; replace its reviewer and retain both decision histories; with self-review allowed, have one learning administrator author and publish a fix and find it labeled self-reviewed in history, then refuse the same when self-review is off.

- **ORG-004 — Give managers only their teams’ learning authority.** Managers assign and follow up their direct or delegated teams, approve due-date exceptions, see team compliance and grant reasoned retries. Cross-team reads/writes and practical assessment decisions outside a separate assessor grant are refused; temporary manager coverage has an end date. The owner names each team's direct managers; a team's members are set by the owner or, for a team in a unit they administer, its learning administrator (ORG-002). Managers do not change team membership, so they never widen their own reach. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in as two team managers, assign and report within each team, try cross-team access and an assessor decision, then exercise and expire a temporary coverage grant.

- **ORG-005 — Support instructors and read-only auditors.** An instructor manages only assigned sessions, rosters and attendance; an auditor reads explicitly shared training evidence for a defined cohort and period. Neither can alter assessments, completions, people or policy; access expires and sensitive files remain individually checked. Today the owner grants instructor access on People for every course or one business unit's courses, and a unit's learning administrator for their own units' courses only, optionally until an end date; grants are revocable and audited and are read on every request. Only an active holder whose access covers the course can be named as a session's instructor (LIVE-001), and an instructor keeps the roster, attendance, evidence and calendar of the sessions they are named on only while that access lasts; their earlier records stay. Instructor access opens no other door: the session itself, assessment rules, assignments, completions (beyond attendance under LIVE-003's revision rule), practical decisions and people stay with the roles that hold them. The owner shares a cohort's evidence with an auditor until a set end: the completions of chosen teams' people within a period, with their certificates and evidence files, each file checked on its own, read-only. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite an instructor and a time-limited auditor through Access, conduct a session and download the allowed evidence, then try prohibited writes and access after expiry.

- **ORG-006 — Preview bulk people changes.** Admins upload CSV or XLSX rosters and bulk invite, update, transfer or deactivate selected employees with validation, field mapping, duplicate handling and a write-free preview. Rows need an email or an employee id; email-less rows map username and mobile columns (as TalentLMS’s Login column does) and get ORG-011 sign-in and first-sign-in sheets instead of emailed invitations. Apply reports per-row outcomes, resumes safely and never deactivates missing rows without an explicitly chosen policy. Exact semantics (sx, 19 Oct): admins are the owner (acting as admin) and a learning administrator (ORG-002) for people in their units’ teams; deactivating (a row whose employment is terminated or inactive, or the missing-people policy) is the owner’s, as access removal is under BEAM-01, marks the person as left (ORG-001) and ends their access, and the missing-people policy reaches only people with an employee id, never the owner or the uploader. Columns map by header name and each mapping can be changed before applying. Every row with a repeated email, employee id or username, a manager employee id found neither in Beam nor in the file, an unknown team, or no email and no employee id is shown and never applied; a row whose employee id and email belong to two people is held as an ORG-001 conflict. The preview writes nothing to people, access or mail; it keeps the uploaded batch for review. Apply takes the selected rows in file order, at most 50 per request, re-checks each row (one whose action changed since the preview is skipped and says so) and resumes where it stopped; managers are linked once every person is in. An invitation goes only to a new person with an email when the uploader chose to invite, and never to someone who already has a sign-in account, so a resumed or repeated apply never invites twice; Base sends at most 30 invitations from one address in 15 minutes, so invitations past that limit are labeled waiting and go out on a later apply. Email-less rows keep their username (stored lowercase, unique) and mobile number and are never emailed; their sign-in and first-sign-in sheets are ORG-011’s, which carries them. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload a 200-person roster with duplicate email, invalid manager, a transfer and email-less rows with usernames, preview without mail/writes, apply twice and resume an interrupted batch without repeated invitations.

- **ORG-007 — Offer an organized self-service learning catalog.** Learners search and browse authorized courses by subject, role, skill, language and duration, see required versus optional work, request approval where needed, self-enroll where allowed and withdraw from optional work without deleting history. Favorites and saved searches remain personal. Today whoever manages a course's catalog (AUTHOR-005: the owner acting as admin, the learning administrator of its unit, or a custom role with the courses permission there) offers it only by assignment (the default), for self-enrollment, or on approval, to everyone or only to chosen teams; a learner is authorized for a course offered to them that is published and neither retired nor archived. The Catalog page and its JSON read search titles, summaries, tags and objectives and filter by subject (the course's folder), skill (one of its tags; LEARN-007 inherits a skill taxonomy), role (a role path whose current revision includes it), language and a longest expected duration in minutes. Required work is anything someone else assigned to the learner; optional work is what they enrolled in themselves or asked for, due 90 days after enrollment at 17:00 in the business time zone. A request waits for whoever may enroll that learner in that course (the owner, their manager for their own teams, the course's unit administrator, an enrollment custom role), who approves it, creating the optional assignment, or declines it with a reason; the learner sees each outcome. Withdrawing ends the learner's own open optional assignment as canceled, kept with its history in their record and the report; required work cannot be withdrawn. Favorites and up to 20 named saved searches belong to their person alone, and a course not offered to someone can be neither enrolled in, requested nor favorited by them. **Acceptance evidence:** As a learner find a course, self-enroll in an open course, request a restricted one, withdraw and inspect history; unauthorized courses and other learners’ favorites stay hidden.

- **ORG-008 — Separate employee and external training portals.** The employer can run branded customer and partner training portals with separate identity, consent, catalogs, enrollment, administrators and reports. Sharing an approved course revision does not share employee rosters, private procedures or cross-portal progress; employees remain the first buyer’s main workflow. Each external portal carries its own ORG-009 theme and hostname. **Acceptance evidence:** Create employee, customer and partner portals, share one course, enroll external learners and test every cross-portal page/API/download and administrator grant for isolation.

- **ORG-009 — Brand the employee portal on the employer’s own address.** The owner sets the employee portal’s theme: logo, primary and accent colors, favicon, sign-in page heading and text, and email sender name. The theme applies to every learner and admin web page, the native apps (LEARN-004), every system email and notification (NOTICE-001, NOTICE-005) and certificates (LEARN-002). The owner also attaches a custom hostname such as `training.example.com`. A setup check verifies DNS, TLS and sign-in callbacks before switching, and the previous address keeps redirecting so printed links and QR codes still work. A theme whose colors fail the LEARN-011 contrast checks is refused with the failing pair named. **Acceptance evidence:** Through Settings, upload a logo, set colors and a favicon and attach a custom hostname; watch the setup check fail on missing DNS, then pass. Sign in at that hostname and see the theme on the learner page, a delivered reminder email and a downloaded certificate. Follow an old assignment link and land on the same assignment. Try a low-contrast color pair and see it refused.

- **ORG-010 — Define custom administrator roles.** Besides the fixed roles, the owner defines named custom roles from checkbox permissions (courses, people, enrollment, reports, live sessions, certificates, integrations, notifications) limited to chosen teams, locations or business units. A custom role never includes owner authority, provider secrets or the named-reviewer and named-assessor decisions, which stay personal grants. Changing a role applies to every holder on their next request, and every grant and change is audited (OPS-001). **Acceptance evidence:** Create a “Site training coordinator” role with enrollment and reports for one location, grant it, then enroll and report in that location. Try another location, a provider credential and a publication, and see each refused. Remove the reports permission and see the open session lose report access.

- **ORG-011 — Sign in without a work email.** Frontline staff with no company mailbox are full Beam learners. A person can exist with no email address, identified by their employee id (ORG-001), whether added by hand, by roster upload (ORG-006), by a file feed (CONNECT-019) or by the HRIS (AUTO-002). The owner switches each sign-in method on or off: an admin-issued username with a password or a numeric PIN of at least six digits, which the person sets on first use from a one-time initial secret; and a one-time code sent by SMS to a verified mobile number from the roster, through the employer’s SMS provider such as Twilio. Both methods work on the web and in the native apps (LEARN-004), and open assignment links from SMS and in-app notices (NOTICE-001, NOTICE-005, LEARN-012). An admin, or a manager for their own team (ORG-004), resets a forgotten password or PIN by issuing a new one-time initial secret; each reset ends the person’s sessions and records who reset whom and when (OPS-001). Failed attempts are rate limited per person and per device; repeated failures lock the account until a cool-down passes or an admin or manager unlocks it. SMS codes expire after 10 minutes by default, which the owner can set between 2 and 15 minutes, and work once. Deactivation, removed access and AUTO-002 leavers end every web and app session, as BEAM-01 does for emailed sign-in. Passwords and PINs are stored only as salted slow hashes, are never shown after issue, and never appear in exports (BEAM-16, OPS-002) or agent reads. Each new hire gets a printable first-sign-in sheet in their LEARN-017 language with the portal address, username, a QR code that opens sign-in with the username filled in, and the initial secret, which expires after a set number of days. A tablet the owner registers as a shared device, such as a break-room tablet, offers quick switching by username and PIN, signs the person out after inactivity or when they leave a course, remembers nobody and keeps no offline downloads (LEARN-005). A person without email never gets email: notices go in-app and by SMS where they have a verified number and have not opted out (LEARN-012); with neither route, the manager’s queue shows “no contact route” for that person (NOTICE-004), never a silent drop. The owner and learning administrators (ORG-002) keep emailed or workplace sign-in for owner recovery; learners, managers, assessors and instructors may use either method. **Acceptance evidence:** Through People, import a roster row with an employee id, a mobile number and no email, and see AUTO-002 create the person and enroll them in their role path. Print their first-sign-in sheet and open its QR code. Sign in on the web and in the iOS and Android apps once with the username and first-use password and once with an SMS code; complete an assigned quiz and download the certificate. As their manager, reset the password, find the reset in Audit (OPS-001) and see the earlier session ended. Exceed the failed-attempt limit and see the lockout, then unlock it. Deactivate the person and see the app session end. Trigger an overdue reminder and see SMS and in-app delivery; remove the mobile number and see “no contact route” in the manager’s queue. On a registered shared tablet, sign in two people one after the other by username and PIN and see the first signed out with nothing of theirs left on the device. Let an SMS code pass its expiry and see it refused. Switch the SMS method off and see SMS codes refused.

- **ORG-012 — View Beam as a learner, read-only.** To troubleshoot, a learning administrator (ORG-002, ORG-010) or a manager for their own team opens “View as” for one learner within their authority and sees the learner pages exactly as that person sees them: assignments, progress, attempts and results, certificates, notices and language. The view is read-only: every write, form, download of the learner’s uploaded evidence and sign-in action is refused, and a banner names whose view it is. - **AUTHOR-003 — Collect expert feedback before publication.** Experts coauthor with conflict detection, comment on specific lessons and questions, resolve review threads and request changes. Learner feedback and broken-content reports reach the responsible expert without disclosing private attempts to all authors. Today everyone who may edit the course (the owner, author-role holders, the course's own authors under ORG-003 and its unit's learning administrator) coauthors its one draft. A lesson save carries the lesson as its editor saw it: saved from an older draft, it applies when nobody changed that lesson meanwhile, and otherwise both texts are kept as a conflict that a coauthor resolves by keeping the current text, using the other text or saving a merged text, recorded with who resolved it and how; any other save from an older draft is refused as stale. - **AUTHOR-004 — Compare and restore earlier content safely.** Authors compare source, lesson and assessment revisions and restore an earlier revision into a new draft. - **AUTHOR-005 — Organize and reuse the course estate.** Admins maintain folders, tags, learning objectives, languages, duration, owners, templates and reusable courses. Bulk retag adds and removes tags on up to 200 courses at once and changes nothing unless every course is one the person manages. - **AUTHOR-010 — Carry published fixes to learners in progress.** When the named reviewer publishes a new revision, they choose for the course’s open assignments: keep each on its revision, or move them to the new one. Moving keeps progress on lessons whose content is unchanged, reopens changed or added lessons, and voids unfinished attempts on changed assessments while keeping them in history. Passed attempts on unchanged assessments carry over. Each moved assignment records both revisions, who moved it and when, and its eventual completion binds the revision actually completed (BEAM-09). Finished learners keep their original completion untouched. When the reviewer marks the change material (BEAM-14), they may also create new assignments for finished learners, AUTO-004-style, with a chosen due date; this never rewrites or revokes the old completion or certificate. The preview before publishing shows who moves, which lessons reopen and who gets a new assignment. Today the publication form offers three choices: keep (open assignments stay on their revision), move the open assignments, or move them and also reassign everyone who finished an earlier revision, which marks the change material (recorded with the revision as its reassignment choice) with a due date that many days ahead. Before publishing, the named reviewer or the owner sees on the revision page (and at `revisions/ID/moves`) each open assignment on an earlier revision with its learner, the lessons it keeps and those that reopen and what happens to its attempts, and who has finished an earlier revision. A lesson is unchanged when its title, text and cited source versions are identical; an assessment is unchanged when its questions, pass mark, attempt limit, time limit, practical instructions and named assessor are identical. Moving ends the earlier assignment as replaced and opens one on the new revision with the same due date, path, prerequisite, renewal interval and access window, recording both revisions, who moved it and when; unchanged lessons the learner completed stay complete with their original times, and the learner resumes at the first lesson left. With an unchanged assessment every attempt carries over: a pass stays passed, numbering and the attempt limit continue, and a practical waiting for its assessor stays waiting and, once accepted, completes the new assignment. With a changed assessment earlier attempts no longer count, and the unfinished ones (a practical waiting for a decision, an open timed quiz) are voided: kept with their evidence under a move entry in the earlier assignment's history and never decided. Session attendance (LIVE-003) follows the assignment through moves. **Acceptance evidence:** With one learner mid-course and one finished, publish a typo fix and choose “move”: the mid-course learner keeps their progress at the same place. Then publish a revision with an added lesson, marked material, choosing “move” and “reassign finished”: the mid-course learner sees only the new lesson reopened, the finished learner’s original completion and certificate are unchanged, and a new assignment appears for them. Repeat the publication with “keep” and see no assignment move or new assignment.

### Learning, assessment and mobile

- **LEARN-001 — Configure assessments and course feedback.** Authors build question banks with randomized ordering and random draws from a pool, weighted questions, multiple choice, the LEARN-015 fill-in-the-blank, ordering and matching types, free response and file assignments, imported GIFT/Aiken banks (LEARN-016), pass thresholds, attempt limits, feedback timing and surveys. Assessment versions retain answers/scoring and manual grading authority; satisfaction surveys stay separate from passing rules. Exact semantics: a course's questions are its bank; an attempt asks every question in the author's order, or the set number of questions, every question marked "always ask" among them and the rest drawn at random from the others, in the bank's order or shuffled. The draw is fixed by the assignment and attempt number, so reopening an attempt never redraws, and each attempt records the questions it asked. A question weighs 1 to 100 points (1 when unset) and earns its whole weight only when every part of its answer is right; a written (free-response) answer earns what the named assessor awards, 0 to its weight (an empty one earns 0), and its attempt waits for that grading before it passes, fails or allows another attempt. Percent = points earned × 100 / points possible, rounded down, against the pass mark. One named assessor per assessment revision grades written answers and practical (file) evidence. Feedback timing is set per revision: learners see which questions they got right after each attempt, only once they passed or have no attempts left, or never (score only); their managers and the named assessor always see it. The satisfaction survey (1–5 ratings and written questions) is part of the reviewed revision, optional for learners once every lesson is done, answered once per assignment, stored without the learner's name and never read by passing, attempts or completion; a revision's results show only from three answers, to the owner, the course's responsible expert (catalog owner, else named reviewer), its unit's learning administrator and its authors, never to managers, assessors, auditors or custom roles. - **LEARN-015 — Score fill-in, ordering and matching questions.** Besides multiple choice and free response, assessments offer fill-in-the-blank (one or more blanks, each with accepted answers, optional case-insensitivity and trimmed whitespace), ordering (put items in sequence) and matching (pair items, including drag-and-drop with a keyboard equivalent). Attendance only completes the exact required revision under the reviewed program policy; mixed online/offline requirements remain separate. Today the program policy is part of the course revision: its author may require attending a session of the course for a share (1 to 100%) of the session's scheduled minutes (meeting length times meetings), which the reviewer sees and publishes with the revision. Once a session's first meeting has begun, its named instructor or whoever schedules it (LIVE-001) records for each registered learner attended (every scheduled minute), partly attended (the minutes attended) or absent (no minutes, never complete), a completion decision, a note and an optional evidence file. Nobody records their own attendance, and each learner has one current record per session. The roster and its timeline belong to the session's instructor and schedulers; the learner and their managers, for the members of their own teams (as with offline-session evidence), read the learner's attendance records with every amendment and download their evidence files, an ORG-005 auditor reads a file only through the per-file share, and anyone else is refused. Corrections are amendments: a new record with a required reason supersedes the current one; nothing is edited or deleted, and the roster's timeline lists every record with who made it and when, as the audit (OPS-001) does. The requirement is met by the current record for the learner's assignment when its decision is complete and its minutes reach the share. Attendance never completes lessons or an assessment, and they never stand in for attendance: the course completes on the exact revision of that assignment only when its lessons, its assessment and its attendance are all met, and a revision without the requirement is never completed by attendance. ### Agent work beyond course generation

- **AGENT-001 — Connect work errors to reviewed learning sources.** With manager-approved data scopes, the agent joins procedure versions, learning/attempt evidence and operational error patterns from authorized Zendesk, Jira, Salesforce Service Cloud or owner-supplied CSV/API records. It minimizes/redacts customer and employee detail, cites exact inputs and surfaces missing/conflicting signals before proposing a diagnosis. **Acceptance evidence:** Authorize a scoped support/error feed, select a training cohort and request diagnosis; inspect source ids/redactions, reject an out-of-scope record and revoke the connection without retaining unauthorized searchable text.

- **AGENT-002 — Distinguish training gaps from broken processes.** The agent compares observed error patterns, procedure clarity, prior completions and staff questions to propose training, clearer instructions, a system/process fix or no intervention, with reasons and uncertainty. A manager approves voluntary questions before delivery and reviewers keep publication/assessment decisions; anonymous answers never become staff ratings. **Acceptance evidence:** Give the agent one genuine knowledge gap and one unusable process with trained staff, review its cited alternatives, approve a voluntary question plan and reject an unsupported training recommendation.

- **AGENT-003 — Propose the smallest useful intervention.** From a reviewed diagnosis the agent proposes a short clarification, targeted practice, an assessor session, a limited cohort assignment or a process change, with expected benefit, affected roles, paid-time effort and exclusions. The manager approves the exact plan and a content reviewer publishes changes; the agent cannot choose broader mandatory training silently. **Acceptance evidence:** Review an error diagnosis affecting one role, inspect a minimal intervention against a broad-course alternative and approve only the selected people/actions; reject a changed payload and inspect decision lineage.

- **AGENT-004 — Maintain only learning affected by a procedure change.** The agent compares immutable procedure versions to cited lesson passages, quiz answers, practice rubrics and package metadata, identifies invalidated teaching and affected roles/renewal dates, and drafts only necessary changes. The named reviewer decides materiality, publication and retraining; earlier evidence remains immutable and stale proposals are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Change one procedure step, request an impact proposal and inspect affected passages/questions/roles versus untouched content; decline retraining, then approve a revised exact proposal and compare old completions.

- **AGENT-005 — Measure whether learning transfers into work.** Before an intervention, the manager chooses operational outcome, comparison period, cohort exposure, baseline and privacy bounds. The agent compares before/after outcomes and a suitable comparison group when available, showing missing data, sample limits, confounders and uncertainty; completion proves neither competence nor causation. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve a metric and baseline through the intervention page, import scoped pre/post outcomes with missing rows and a process change, then inspect the comparison and refusal to assert causation or rank employees.

## Honest boundaries

Beam is US-first adult workforce learning, not an accrediting body, HRIS,
payroll engine, school SIS, course marketplace, videoconferencing service or
specialist proctoring business. Employers choose applicable training with
qualified content/legal/safety reviewers. Library metadata, seat time and
evidence help meet that decision; a Beam certificate never guarantees statutory
compliance, work eligibility or competence. Safety and health-data requirements
are implementation and agreement obligations in LIB-002 through LIB-004, not
scope exclusions. Specialized proctoring or official credentials use qualified
providers and retain their provenance.

Google/Microsoft/Okta identity, HRIS connections, Slack/Teams apps, mobile app
stores, mail, video, model and licensed-content accounts require employer
authorization, provider permissions and any necessary agreements. Beam shows
what is connected, unavailable, failed or awaiting approval. Provider
subscriptions and content licenses are separately disclosed in USD. A buyer
may choose native package support or an authorized player/LRS connection; both
must meet the same evidence and learner outcomes. Beam does not build a new
Zoom, video CDN, payroll product or library publisher to obtain those services.

Current local file admissions (25 MB source, 8 MB practical evidence and
10 MB per import file with five TalentLMS course reports) are implementation
limits, not target capacity. Larger media/packages and 200-employee operation
remain obligations under LEARN-006 and OPS-008. The earlier Australian pilot
and first-release exclusions do not limit this owner-approved US destination.
The Beam naming collision recorded in the previous contract remains a launch
naming decision to resolve; this document does not rename the product.
